This app was built on a home lab. Its measurement logs are private (they describe someone's home network), so this
copy summarizes the numbers the "A real home rig" lesson uses. Names, addresses and hostnames are left out on purpose.

Machines
- Workstation: two NVIDIA RTX 3090 graphics cards (24 GB each), 256 GB of DDR5 RAM (4 x 64 GB), running a large
  mixture-of-experts language model at 8-bit (Q8_0) with llama.cpp, most expert weights in system RAM.
- Small server: one 16 GB graphics card running a 27B-class model at about 55 tokens per second with a 96K-token context.

Tuning (workstation, generation speed in tokens per second, llama.cpp benchmark scripts)
- Out of the box: 14
- A few expert layers placed on the GPUs, multi-token-prediction (MTP) draft model for speculative decoding: 20.5
- RAM speed raised from 4,400 to 5,200 MT/s: 21.7 (measured RAM bandwidth at 4,400 MT/s: about 62 GB/s)
- Best stable GPU overclock: 21.8 -> 21.9-22.0
- Undervolt (clock locked at 1,800 MHz with an offset): same speed, about 15 W less per GPU

Incidents
- 2026-10-14: a GPU stress test with a memory overclock of +3000 hung the card (driver error Xid 119). The stuck test kept
  the GPU at about 397 W and 83 degrees C (core) for about 10 hours until the heat was noticed and the machine rebooted.
- 2026-10-15: at stock settings, a stress test pushed one RTX 3090's memory to 104 degrees C within about 2.5 minutes while
  the core read 75 degrees C. 12 of the card's 24 memory chips are on the back, cooled only by the backplate.
- 2026-10-18: a tripped breaker reset the BIOS and re-enabled Secure Boot; the unsigned NVIDIA kernel module was refused
  and the GPUs disappeared until the setting was changed and saved in a BIOS profile (with "restore on AC power loss").

Splitting across two GPUs (small server, 2026-10-22/23; added 2026-10-28 for the "Splitting a model across GPUs" lesson)
- The small server's 16 GB card plus a second, older 12 GB card were tried together with llama.cpp.
- A 27B dense model at 4 bits, tensor split across both cards: 39-47 tokens/s writing, 658-807 tokens/s reading.
  The cards are different generations, so it needed llama.cpp's own all-reduce (NCCL failed) and a smaller batch
  (-ub 512, the 12 GB card ran out of memory otherwise).
- A 27B model squeezed to about 2.4 bits on the 16 GB card alone: about 55 tokens/s.
- A 74B mixture-of-experts model (9B active) with the default layer split, 14.0 GB + 10.4 GB: 52.6 tokens/s.

A notes helper built with RAG (Oct 2026; added 2026-10-28 for the "Build your own notes helper" lesson)
- Topic: a radio hobby (long-range mesh radios) for a beginner. Collected material: official docs, papers, guides,
  hardware pages and forum Q&A, cleaned into 1,754 text files in 5 knowledge bases in Open WebUI.
- Settings: chunks of 1,500 characters with 150 overlapping, split at Markdown headings; a small open embedding model
  (bge-base-en-v1.5); hybrid search (keywords + meaning); top 10 passages reranked on the CPU to the best 6
  (bge-reranker-base). The helper answers with numbered sources.
- First try, a big model on the workstation searching all 5 knowledge bases: 573 seconds for one question.
- Moved to a 27B model on the small server's 16 GB GPU: about 90 seconds per answer.
- Test: 25 beginner questions, each with key facts checked by hand against five curated notes; another local model
  grades how many key facts each answer contains (it is noisy by a few points).
  - Starting point: score 0.80, median answer 74 s (69 s before the first word appeared).
  - The five hand-checked notes (~27 KB) put in the helper's instructions: score 0.91, median 65 s. (The questions
    came from those notes, so this test favours the change.)
  - About 90% of each answer's time came before the first word: an extra model call to rewrite the question into
    search queries, the search, reranking on the CPU, and reading ~20,000 tokens of passages. Writing took 5-10 s.
  - Extra query-writing step turned off, top 5 passages reranked to the best 3: score 0.89 (within the grader's noise),
    median 20 s, slowest 26 s (was 93 s). Every answer still cited sources.
